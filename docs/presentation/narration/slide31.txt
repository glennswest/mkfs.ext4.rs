The second epilogue is a measurement. Unpacking one file of nine point seven megabytes cost five point four gigabytes of device I O. One point three seven million operations. Two hundred and eighty times write amplification — over a thousand times at the worst.
And the device was never the problem. Point oh eight milliseconds per operation. Every few kilobytes of payload re-read and re-wrote the same bitmap, inode table, extent node and superblock blocks — the device was acting as the only metadata cache.
Transport fixes were tried first, and they acquitted the lower layers. Coalescing and reordering barely moved it.
[beat=0.5] The fix sits at the seam the design left for it: a write-back cache over the block device trait, with sub-block dirty tracking, so a partial write to an uncached block pays no read-modify-write.
